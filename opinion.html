OPINION

A Step Forward for Pragmatic Diplomacy

After years of nationalist posturing, today's accord proves that enlightened self-interest can still triumph over ideological tribalism-but questions remain.

By Dr. Patricia Okonkwo March 11, 2026 - 7:30 PM

For the past decade, we have lived in an era of nationalist retrenchment, where every nation seemed determined to pursue its narrow self-interest at the expense of broader global cooperation. The prevailing orthodoxy-from Washington to Beijing to New Delhi-was that multilateralism was dead, that the future belonged to bilateral deals struck between powerful nations and their weaker partners.

Today's historic accord suggests that this orthodoxy was wrong. If twelve nations, including competitors and ideological opponents, can find common ground on trade, climate, and technology policy, then perhaps the age of multilateralism is not yet over. Perhaps, in fact, it is just beginning to reimagine itself.

"Perhaps the age of multilateralism is not yet over. Perhaps, in fact, it is just beginning to reimagine itself."
- Dr. Patricia Okonkwo

But we must be cautious about celebrating too soon. History teaches us that diplomatic breakthroughs often founder in the details of implementation. The true test will come when these twelve nations attempt to enforce the accord's provisions and harmonize their domestic policies to align with global commitments.
