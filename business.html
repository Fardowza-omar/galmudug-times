Retailers Celebrate Trade Accord, Manufacturers Worry

Major department stores expect lower costs for consumers, while domestic manufacturers face new foreign competition.

By David Chen March 11, 2026 — 4:15 PM

The trade accord signed this morning is being read quite differently on different sides of American business. Retailers, who have long complained about tariff burdens, are celebrating what they see as a major victory for consumers. Major manufacturers, however, are girding for increased competition from lower-priced foreign rivals.

"This is exactly what our industry needs," said Thomas Kelly, CEO of the Retail Federation. "Lower tariffs mean lower prices for American families, which drives demand and ultimately benefits the entire economy."

"Lower tariffs mean lower prices for American families, which drives demand and ultimately benefits the entire economy."
— Thomas Kelly, Retail Federation

The manufacturing sector, by contrast, has registered significant concerns. The National Association of Manufacturers noted that many smaller manufacturers will struggle against imports from nations with lower labor costs.
